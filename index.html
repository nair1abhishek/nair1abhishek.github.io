Example post · building

Starting small, building often

A good project doesn't have to begin with a grand plan. Sometimes the best first step is a tiny experiment: make one thing work, write down what surprised you, and decide what to try next.

Small projects create a useful rhythm. They make progress visible, leave room for curiosity, and give you something real to improve.

Example post · terminal

A few commands worth remembering

A terminal is a great place to keep simple tasks simple. For example, these commands help you find your bearings in a project:

pwd          # where am I?
ls -la       # what's here?
git status   # what has changed?

Start with the commands you use often, and keep a short note for the ones you always look up.

Example post · notes

Keep a notebook for your future self

When you solve a tricky problem, write down the context and the fix. A short note about why something worked can be more useful than a long record of every step.

Over time, those notes become a map of what you've learned—and a helpful starting point for the next problem.
